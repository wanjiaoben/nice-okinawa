Workplace Japanese and Cross-Cultural Training in Okinawa: Five Practical Business Scenarios

A workplace communication problem is not always a lack of Japanese vocabulary. Unclear instructions, unconfirmed requests and inconsistent handovers can create confusion between Japanese and international staff. Catalina Japan designs practical workplace Japanese and cross-cultural communication support around the conversations that happen every day.

Business training scenarios · Catalina Japan株式会社

The following situations are fictional examples created to explain possible uses of our services. They do not describe actual companies, customers, past assignments, or confirmed results.

Five hypothetical workplace scenarios

01Hotels and accommodation

The morning handover sounds clear—but key details get missed

Practice concise guest-request handovers, room status updates, and “repeat back and confirm” phrases shared by reception and housekeeping teams.

02Restaurants and food service

Orders and instructions get vague during the rush

Train front-of-house and kitchen teams to confirm changes, convey important allergy and hygiene information, and clarify shift instructions. Language training does not replace operational safety procedures.

03Care and community support

Staff hesitate to ask a second question

Build language for handovers, reporting changes and asking for clarification. This communication course does not replace regulated professional or mandatory care training.

04International recruitment and support

Different receiving employers explain things differently

Create reusable staff-facing explanations, practical phrase sheets and participation records for support organizations working with multiple receiving employers.

05Tourism and local services

Teams switch between Japanese, English and Chinese

Simplify reservation changes, calls, chat support, safety notices and internal handovers with short multilingual guides and role-play.

How we can help

We start with a real communication challenge, not a generic language syllabus. Programs can begin with a small, focused workshop.

Needs assessment

Identify job roles, working languages, Japanese levels and recurring misunderstandings.

Workplace Japanese and plain-language training

Reporting, confirming, polite expressions and role-play for international and Japanese staff.

Translation and multilingual support

Adapt short workplace guides, onboarding explanations and routine internal documents.

Materials and follow-up

Role-specific phrase sheets, participation records and simple comprehension checks. Optional digital learning can be discussed separately.

Frequently asked questions

We do not have a course plan. Can we still ask?

Yes. Tell us the job roles and an example of a communication problem. We can outline appropriate training topics and delivery options.

Can Japanese employees attend too?

Yes. We can cover how Japanese colleagues give clearer instructions, check understanding and support two-way communication.

Can the training be online only?

We can discuss digital materials, but delivery suitability and subsidy eligibility are separate questions.

Do you guarantee any grant or subsidy?

No. The program administrator decides eligibility and awards. We can discuss the scope of a training outline or quotation.

Tell us one communication problem your team faces.

Share your industry, approximate team size, working languages and a typical challenge. We can suggest a practical training or translation approach.
