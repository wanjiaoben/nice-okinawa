Japan and Vietnam Sign MOC on Employment for Skill Development: Employers Can Prepare Job-Specific Japanese Training for 2027

2026-11-13 | Shinko Lab

Japan and Vietnam signed a Memorandum of Cooperation on the Employment for Skill Development Program on November 9, 2026. Japan’s Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, Immigration Services Agency, and Organization for Technical Intern Training subsequently updated their official information. Vietnam is now among seven countries covered by this cooperation framework.

The new program is scheduled to begin on April 1, 2027. It is designed to develop and secure workers for sectors facing labor shortages in Japan. Cooperation under the MOC began on the signing date and is set to continue for five years, with automatic five-year renewal unless either side gives the required notice.

For employers planning to receive workers from Vietnam, the MOC is not an individual visa approval and does not remove sector-specific or plan-approval requirements. Immigration guidance for pre-enforcement applications states that Vietnam is one of the countries for which an official recommendation letter must be submitted for each applicant. Receiving organizations must also check the relevant sector council, field-specific standards, employment conditions, and approval requirements for the employment development plan.

OTIT’s current official page lists a provisional Vietnamese sending-organization list. Employers should still confirm the latest list, sector rules, and document requirements at the time of application.

From a workforce-development perspective, employers can begin with three preparations now: define the job and actual duties; assess each candidate’s current Japanese level; and connect pre-employment basic Japanese, post-entry job-specific Japanese, and workplace communication training. In tourism, accommodation, food service, care, manufacturing, and other fields, training should be tied directly to work tasks such as safety checks, reporting and consultation, customer explanations, and reading workplace documents.
