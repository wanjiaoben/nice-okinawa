Declared for South Korea, Bound for Russia: Compliance Lessons from Japan’s 73-Vehicle Export Case

2026-11-23 · CATALINA JAPAN K.K.

On 4 November 2026, Kobe Customs announced that two cases involving the alleged unlawful export of used vehicles and related goods to Russia had been referred to prosecutors on 16 and 23 October for suspected Customs Act violations. The cases involved 45 and 28 vehicles and related goods, for a total of 73. According to the official notice, false export declarations listed the Republic of Korea as the destination even though the goods were allegedly intended for Russia, using exporters and customs brokers who were unaware of the scheme.

The announcement does not mean that every used vehicle is prohibited from export to Russia, nor that every shipment routed through South Korea is unlawful. Applicability depends on the HS code, engine displacement, powertrain, vehicle category and end use. Japan’s Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry also warns that declaring a third country as the destination while knowing that the goods will ultimately be unloaded in Russia may constitute an unapproved export intended to circumvent sanctions. Certain trucks, passenger vehicles with engines over 1,900 cc, hybrid passenger vehicles and other categories may be restricted.

Before the next shipment, exporters should verify:

  1. the true final destination and end user;
  2. consistency among the consignee, beneficial controller and payer;
  3. whether any third-country routing is necessary and adequately explained;
  4. the vehicle’s HS code, engine size, powertrain and intended use;
  5. consistency among the invoice, bill of lading, export declaration and bank-transfer records; and
  6. whether shipment has been paused for expert or authority review when documents and customer explanations conflict.

The core issue is not transshipment through South Korea itself. It is whether the declared destination matches the actual final destination. Compliance checks should be completed before loading, not reconstructed after a warning sign appears.
